Privacy Policy

Applies to Noto for iPad. Last updated 20 October 2026.

The short version

Signing in is optional, Noto has no servers of its own, and there are no analytics. Your notes are files on your iPad and are never uploaded anywhere. Noto uses the network only for things you start: reading a calendar feed you added yourself, playing a YouTube video you paste into a note and then tap play on, and signing in with Apple or Google if you choose to.

What Noto stores

Your notes, their pages, any PDFs and images you import, your settings and, if you sign in, your profile — all inside Noto’s own container on this device, apart from the search entries described under Search on your iPad and the small sign-in record described under Signing in with Apple or Google below. Deleting the app deletes the container.

Search on your iPad

Noto adds each note’s title, the name of the folder it is in, its course code (if it has one) and the text in it to your iPad’s on-device Spotlight index, so you can find a note from the iPad’s system search without opening Noto. The text is what you typed, the words Noto reads from your handwriting on this iPad, and the text of PDFs you imported. The index is kept by iPadOS on this iPad: Noto does not send it anywhere, it is not part of a Noto backup or export, and a note you move to Recently Deleted is taken out of it.

You can turn this off for Noto in iPad Settings > Siri & Search (or Settings > Apps > Noto). Your notes themselves are not changed.

Signing in with Apple or Google

Noto works fully without signing in. If you choose to sign in with Apple or Google, it is so that Home can greet you by name and Settings > Account can show your photo, on this iPad. Noto has no account system of its own and no Noto password; your password stays with Apple or Google.

What Noto keeps: the identifier Apple or Google gives Noto for your account, the email address it shares (if it shares one), a display name and, if you pick one, a profile photo. The display name starts from the name the provider shares, and you can change it. The photo is one you choose: iPadOS shows you the picker and gives Noto only that photo, so Noto never asks for access to your photo library, and Noto shrinks it and strips its location and other embedded details before saving it. All of this stays on this iPad. It is not synced, it is not part of a Noto backup or export, it is not sent to us, and your notes are not tied to it.

Apple or Google handles the sign-in itself, and their privacy terms cover that part. If you sign in with Google, Google’s sign-in library keeps its sign-in token on this iPad. While you are signed in, each time Noto opens or comes back to the screen it asks Apple or Google whether that sign-in is still valid; if you have removed Noto from your Apple or Google account, Noto signs you out on this iPad. That check carries nothing about your notes.

Sign out hides your name and photo until you sign in again; they stay on this iPad. Remove profile erases the name and photo from this iPad. Neither one touches your notes or your Apple or Google account. To end Noto’s sign-in at the provider’s end as well, remove Noto from the Sign in with Apple list in your Apple Account settings, or from the third-party connections in your Google Account. Change password in Settings > Account opens your Apple or Google account page, because that is where your password lives.

Noto keeps a small record of which account is signed in, in this iPad’s keychain, which iPadOS can keep after the app is deleted. If you reinstall Noto, it clears that record the first time it opens.

What Noto collects

Nothing. There is no usage tracking, no crash reporting and no advertising identifier. Noto is built with Apple’s own frameworks plus one outside library, Google Sign-In, which is used only if you sign in with Google; the Acknowledgements list it and the open-source code it relies on.

That is a statement about the app. If you choose to contact us, what you send reaches a person — see Getting help below.

Calendar feeds

If you add a calendar feed — a Canvas feed link, a Google Calendar iCal address, or any other .ics URL — Noto downloads that address over HTTPS to show your events beside your notes, and refreshes it when you open Noto and while you are using it, for as long as you keep it. The request goes to the address you typed and nowhere else, it carries nothing about you or your notes, and the events are cached on this device. Remove the feed and Noto stops making the request.

YouTube videos in your notes

If you paste a YouTube link into a note, Noto shows it as a card — no network request happens yet. Tap play, and Noto loads that video from YouTube (youtube-nocookie.com), right there on the page. That request goes to YouTube, carries nothing about your notes, and only happens when you tap play; the card itself works offline, but the video won’t play without a connection. YouTube’s own privacy terms cover that request, the same way they would if you opened the same video in Safari.

Add no calendar feed, play no video and don’t sign in, and Noto never touches the network at all.

Permissions Noto may ask for

Your device’s Calendar, only if you turn on the calendar strip, and only to read events to show beside your notes — that is a local read, separate from the feed above. Notifications, only if you turn on due reminders for a connected calendar feed — Noto schedules a reminder the evening before and the morning of an assignment’s due date, entirely on this device, from the feed you already added; nothing about a reminder is sent anywhere. The microphone, only when you start a recording inside a note; recordings are saved with that note on this device. The camera, only when you scan paper pages into a note; the scanned pages are saved in that note on this device. Noto never asks for any of these until you use the feature, and declining leaves the rest of the app working.

Backups and things you send out yourself

Backup, export and share are yours to aim. When you export a PDF or save a backup, it goes wherever you choose — Files, iCloud Drive, another app — and from that point it is covered by that destination’s terms, not this one.

Getting help

If you open a support request or post in the Noto Discord, we see what you send: your GitHub or Discord username, and whatever you describe. Support requests are public GitHub issues, so keep anything private out of them. That is correspondence, not something the app collected — it is never linked to your notes, because Noto has no way to identify them and never sends them anywhere.

For a problem with a purchase we may ask for an App Store order ID, because that is what Apple needs to look it up — send it privately in the Noto Discord, never in a public support request. Do not send passwords; nobody here will ever ask for one.

GitHub and Discord each have their own privacy terms, which cover their side of the conversation. Ask us to delete a support thread and we will.

Purchases

Noto Plus is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and tells this app only whether the purchase is active. Noto never sees your payment details.

Children

Noto collects nothing from anyone, so there is nothing for it to collect from a child either. It shows no ads and has no social or messaging features.

Changes

If this ever stops being true, it will change in an app update and this page will change with it in the same release.

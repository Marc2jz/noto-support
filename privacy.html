Privacy Policy

Applies to Noto for iPad. Last updated 30 October 2026.

The short version

Signing in is optional (you need it only to buy Noto Plus), Noto has no servers of its own, and there are no analytics. Your notes are files on your iPad. Noto uploads them nowhere unless you turn on iCloud sync or choose Google Drive for Auto-Backup, both optional and off until you do. iCloud sync keeps a copy in your own iCloud, not with us, and we cannot read it. Google Drive Auto-Backup keeps a copy in your own Google Drive, straight from your iPad to Google, and we cannot read that either. If you turn on Auto-Backup, it copies your notes to one place at a time: either a Files folder you choose, and what happens to that folder afterwards is up to the app that provides it, or your own Google Drive. Noto uses the network only for things you start: keeping your iCloud copy up to date if you turned on sync, backing up to and restoring from your own Google Drive if you chose it, reading a calendar feed you added yourself, playing a YouTube video you paste into a note and then tap play on, getting Apple’s speech model the first time you ask for a transcript, and signing in with Apple or Google if you choose to.

What Noto stores

Your notes, their pages, any PDFs and images you import, your settings and, if you sign in, your profile — all inside Noto’s own container on this device, apart from the search entries described under Search on your iPad, the small sign-in record described under Signing in with Apple or Google below, the identifier described under Auto-Backup to a Files folder, the copies Auto-Backup writes to a folder you choose or to your own Google Drive, the small records described under Auto-Backup to Google Drive, and, if you turn on iCloud sync, the copies described under iCloud sync. Deleting the app deletes the container, not your iCloud copy or your Google Drive copy.

Search on your iPad

Noto adds each note’s title, the name of the folder it is in, its course code (if it has one) and the text in it to your iPad’s on-device Spotlight index, so you can find a note from the iPad’s system search without opening Noto. The text is what you typed, the words Noto reads from your handwriting on this iPad, and the text of PDFs you imported. The index is kept by iPadOS on this iPad: Noto does not send it anywhere, it is not part of a Noto backup or export, and a note you move to Recently Deleted is taken out of it.

You can turn this off for Noto in iPad Settings > Siri & Search (or Settings > Apps > Noto). Your notes themselves are not changed.

Signing in with Apple or Google

Noto works fully without signing in; the one thing that needs it is buying Noto Plus (see Purchases). If you choose to sign in with Apple or Google, it is so that Home can greet you by name and Settings > Account can show your photo, on this iPad. Noto has no account system of its own and no Noto password; your password stays with Apple or Google.

What Noto keeps: the identifier Apple or Google gives Noto for your account, the email address it shares (if it shares one), a display name and, if you pick one, a profile photo. The display name starts from the name the provider shares, and you can change it. The photo is one you choose: iPadOS shows you the picker and gives Noto only that photo, so Noto never asks for access to your photo library, and Noto shrinks it and strips its location and other embedded details before saving it. All of this stays on this iPad. iCloud sync does not carry it, it is not part of a Noto backup or export, it is not sent to us, and your notes are not tied to it.

Apple or Google handles the sign-in itself, and their privacy terms cover that part. If you sign in with Google, Google’s sign-in library keeps its sign-in token on this iPad. While you are signed in, each time Noto opens or comes back to the screen it asks Apple or Google whether that sign-in is still valid; if you have removed Noto from your Apple or Google account, Noto signs you out on this iPad. That check carries nothing about your notes.

Sign out hides your name and photo until you sign in again; they stay on this iPad. Remove profile erases the name and photo from this iPad. Neither one touches your notes or your Apple or Google account. To end Noto’s sign-in at the provider’s end as well, remove Noto from the Sign in with Apple list in your Apple Account settings, or from the third-party connections in your Google Account. Change password in Settings > Account opens your Apple or Google account page, because that is where your password lives.

Noto keeps a small record of which account is signed in, in this iPad’s keychain, which iPadOS can keep after the app is deleted. If you reinstall Noto, it clears that record the first time it opens.

What Noto collects

Noto itself collects nothing: there is no usage tracking, no crash reporting and no advertising identifier, and nothing you write or record is ever sent to us. Noto is built with Apple’s own frameworks plus one outside library, Google Sign-In, which runs only if you sign in with Google or choose Google Drive for Auto-Backup (or restore from it); the Acknowledgements list it and the open-source code it relies on. Noto itself shares nothing about you with any third party. Auto-Backup writes only into the folder you choose or to your own Google Drive (see Auto-Backup to Google Drive), and iCloud sync, if you turn it on, writes only to your own iCloud (see iCloud sync); the one thing Noto adds to an App Store purchase is described under Purchases.

Google declares, in that library’s own privacy manifest, that it may collect your name, email address, phone number, coarse location, a user ID, a device ID, usage data and other data, linked to your Google account and not used for tracking, to run the sign-in and for Google’s analytics. Noto’s App Store privacy label lists those types for that reason, although none of it reaches us; Google’s privacy policy covers it. Sign in with Apple, or don’t sign in, and don’t choose or restore from Google Drive, and that library never runs.

That is a statement about the app. If you choose to contact us, what you send reaches a person — see Getting help below.

Calendar feeds

If you add a calendar feed — a Canvas feed link, a Google Calendar iCal address, or any other .ics URL — Noto downloads that address over HTTPS to show your events beside your notes, and refreshes it when you open Noto and while you are using it, for as long as you keep it. The request goes to the address you typed and nowhere else, it carries nothing about you or your notes, and the events are cached on this device. Remove the feed and Noto stops making the request.

YouTube videos in your notes

If you paste a YouTube link into a note, Noto shows it as a card — no network request happens yet. Tap play, and Noto loads that video from YouTube (youtube-nocookie.com), right there on the page. That request goes to YouTube, carries nothing about your notes, and only happens when you tap play; the card itself works offline, but the video won’t play without a connection. YouTube’s own privacy terms cover that request, the same way they would if you opened the same video in Safari. The player stays on that one video: a link you tap inside it, such as the video’s title, opens in the YouTube app or Safari, outside Noto.

Transcripts

On iPadOS 26 or later, Noto can turn a recording into text with Apple’s speech recognition, on this iPad. The first time you ask for a transcript in a language, iPadOS downloads Apple’s speech model for it, after you agree, and may update it later; that download comes from Apple and carries nothing about your notes. The recording and its transcript stay with the note on this iPad, apart from any copy you send out yourself, have Auto-Backup make (see Auto-Backup to a Files folder and Auto-Backup to Google Drive) or have iCloud sync keep in your own iCloud (see iCloud sync).

Add no calendar feed, play no video, ask for no transcript, leave iCloud sync off, don’t choose or restore from Google Drive and don’t sign in, and Noto itself makes no network requests. (The App Store may still check your purchases.)

Permissions Noto may ask for

Your device’s Calendar, only if you turn on the calendar strip, and only to read events to show beside your notes — that is a local read, separate from the feed above. Notifications, only if you turn on due reminders for a connected calendar feed — Noto schedules a reminder the evening before and the morning of an assignment’s due date, entirely on this device, from the feed you already added; nothing about a reminder is sent anywhere. The microphone, only when you start a recording inside a note; recordings are saved with that note on this iPad and stay there unless you turn on iCloud sync, or choose a backup folder that syncs and turn on Include recordings, which copies them there, or choose your own Google Drive for Auto-Backup and turn on Include recordings, which uploads them there. The camera, only when you scan paper pages into a note; the scanned pages are saved in that note on this iPad and stay there unless you turn on iCloud sync, choose a backup folder that syncs or choose your own Google Drive for Auto-Backup. Noto sends neither anywhere except, if you turn on iCloud sync, to your own iCloud and, if you choose Google Drive for Auto-Backup, to your own Google Drive. Noto never asks for any of these until you use the feature, and declining leaves the rest of the app working.

Backups and things you send out yourself

Backup, export and share are yours to aim. When you export a PDF or save a backup, it goes wherever you choose — Files, iCloud Drive, another app — and from that point it is covered by that destination’s terms, not this one. Auto-Backup, described next, is the automatic version of the same thing.

Auto-Backup to a Files folder

Auto-Backup is off until you turn it on in Settings > Connected Services, and it is free. It copies your notes into a folder you choose in the Files picker. iPadOS gives Noto access to that one folder, and Noto keeps a bookmark to it, plus a record of what it has already copied, on this iPad.

What it copies: your notes, in the format you pick — a Noto backup (the default, which you can restore from Settings > Export & Backup), a PDF of each note, or both. Recordings are copied only if you turn on Include recordings, which is off by default; recordings over 500 MB are left out. A PDF never includes recordings, and handwriting in a PDF is saved as a picture. Turning Include recordings off removes recordings from the backup copy of each note you still have the next time it is backed up; your provider may keep them in its own trash.

When: Noto tries to back up when you turn Auto-Backup on, a moment after you leave a note, when you leave the app, and when you tap Back Up Now, and also after you change the folder, the format or Include recordings; it retries notes that failed. iPadOS may also give Noto a little time in the background, but it decides when, so a backup is not guaranteed to run at any particular time. When you come back to Noto it checks that it can still reach the folder, and if an earlier backup was left unfinished, or a PDF was waiting for Noto to be open, it tries again.

Where it goes: Noto itself never uploads anything for this. It has no server and makes no network request for Auto-Backup; it writes into the folder. If that folder is in iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Box or another provider’s app, that provider’s app syncs it, under its own terms, and this page does not cover that copy. (Choosing Google Drive in Connected Services is different: see Auto-Backup to Google Drive.) Noto replaces a note’s backup copy when the note changes, and if you rename or move a note it removes the earlier PDF it wrote for it. It never deletes files it did not write, and deleting a note in Noto does not delete its backup. Turning Auto-Backup off makes Noto forget the folder and its record of what it copied, and stop asking iPadOS for background time; the files already in the folder stay until you delete them.

What else it writes: in your folder, if you chose a Noto backup (the default) or both, a Noto Backup folder (your notes, plus folders.json and backup-info.json, which lists the format version, when the backup was made, Noto’s version and build, the number of notes, and a random identifier Noto made for this install) and a Noto Staging folder, whose scratch contents Noto removes when it is done (the folder itself stays, with a subfolder named from the first eight characters of the identifier); and a Noto PDFs folder if you chose PDFs, whose files are named after your notes and sit in folders that mirror yours. When you back up in the Noto backup format, the identifier lets Noto tell whether a folder already holds a backup from a different iPad; if it does, Noto stops and says so. The identifier also names the scratch subfolder inside Noto Staging. You can choose another folder or tap Back Up to a New Folder Inside, which creates a folder named with the iPad’s name as iPadOS reports it to Noto and the first eight characters of the identifier, such as iPad (1a2b3c4d). Noto creates that folder only if you ask.

The same identifier is kept in this iPad’s keychain, set to stay on this device and be readable after the iPad is first unlocked, so a background backup can use it. Noto does not delete it — not when you turn Auto-Backup off and not when you reinstall — so a reinstalled Noto still recognises its own folder; iPadOS can keep keychain items after an app is deleted, so it can remain after Noto is gone. It is a random number and holds nothing about you or your notes. Before copying a note, Noto also reads how much free space this iPad has to be sure the temporary copy fits; it neither stores nor sends that number.

Auto-Backup to Google Drive

Instead of a Files folder, you can choose Google Drive for Auto-Backup in Settings > Connected Services. It is off until you choose it, and it is free. Noto backs up to one place at a time, a Files folder or your own Google Drive, not both. Choosing Google Drive opens Google’s sign-in and consent screen if Google needs it (not if you are already signed in with Google and have given the permission below), run by Google’s sign-in library (the Google Sign-In library described under What Noto collects), and asks for one permission, Google’s drive.file permission. With it, Noto can see only the files it puts in your Google Drive, nothing else in it. If you signed in to Noto with Google, Google Drive has to use that same Google account; choosing a different one on Google’s screen signs you out of Noto on this iPad.

What Noto accesses: your Google account’s user ID and email address, which the sign-in gives Noto, and the files and folders Noto itself creates in your Google Drive. Noto uses them only to back up your notes to your Drive, to restore them, to show the account in Settings and to notice if a different Google account is signed in later. Noto does not share them with anyone. It has no server, so nothing reaches us. Noto keeps them on this iPad and sends them nowhere; the only things it sends to Google are the backup files and folders it creates and the access token.

What it writes: a folder called Noto at the top of your Google Drive, found or made when you choose Google Drive. Inside it, if you chose a Noto backup (the default) or both, a Noto Backup folder with your notes in the same files as the Files format, plus folders.json and backup-info.json, which lists the format version, when the backup was made, Noto’s version and build, the number of notes, and the random identifier Noto made for this install (the same one described under Auto-Backup to a Files folder); and a Noto PDFs folder if you chose PDFs, whose files are named after your notes and sit in folders that mirror yours. There is no Noto Staging folder on Google Drive. Recordings are uploaded only if you turn on Include recordings, which is off by default; recordings over 500 MB are left out. When you back up in the Noto backup format, the identifier lets Noto tell whether the backup already belongs to a different iPad; if it does, Noto stops and says so (see Use This iPad Instead below).

Where it goes: uploads go straight from this iPad to Google. Noto has no server of its own and nothing passes through one. Noto’s own requests go to www.googleapis.com only, Google’s Drive service: it finds and makes folders, lists them, uploads and downloads files and moves files to the trash there. Noto follows no redirect to any other host, keeps no cookies and caches nothing from these requests. Each request carries a short-lived access token from Google’s sign-in library, which Noto does not store. The sign-in itself, and renewing it, is done by Google’s library, which talks to Google’s own sign-in servers (for example accounts.google.com and oauth2.googleapis.com). Google’s terms and privacy policy cover your Drive and your Google account.

When: Noto tries to back up when you choose Google Drive, a moment after you leave a note, when you leave the app and when you tap Back Up Now. iPadOS may also give Noto a little time in the background, but it decides when, so a backup is not guaranteed to run at any particular time. If Noto can’t reach Google, it tries again. Noto replaces a note’s backup when the note changes. When it replaces or removes a file it wrote, it moves the old one to your Google Drive’s trash, where you can restore it; it never permanently deletes a file from your Drive, and it changes only files it created. Deleting a note in Noto does not delete its backup.

Sign Out of Google Drive, in the Google Drive section of Settings > Connected Services, stops Noto backing up to Google Drive, ends the Google sign-in Noto was using for it on this iPad (if you signed in to Noto with Google, that sign-in stays, because Noto still uses it), and makes Noto forget the Google account it recorded and the list described below. It does not delete anything from your Google Drive: the Noto folder and everything in it stay until you delete them. It does not revoke the permission you gave Google; to end Noto’s access at Google’s end, remove Noto from the third-party connections in your Google Account. Turning Auto-Backup off stops backups, but only Sign Out of Google Drive ends the Google sign-in.

If you choose a Files folder instead, Noto asks first, then stops backing up to Google Drive and signs out of it; what is already in Google Drive stays there.

Restore from Google Drive, in Settings > Export & Backup, brings back the notes Auto-Backup copied to your Google Drive. If you are not signed in to Google for Drive, it opens the same Google screen first, without making Google Drive your backup place, then asks you to confirm the account. It downloads your Noto Backup folder into a temporary folder on this iPad and adds those notes to this iPad; notes already on this iPad are not changed. If a note on this iPad differs from the backup’s copy, the backup’s version is added as a separate copy titled “(Restored)” and yours is left as it is; a note you deleted on purpose comes back as a new note. Noto deletes the temporary folder when it finishes, or if it fails. It creates nothing in your Google Drive, and Auto-Backup waits while it runs. If the sign-in was made only for the restore, Noto signs it out afterwards, unless Google Drive has become your backup place in the meantime.

Use This iPad Instead: a Google Drive backup belongs to one iPad, the one whose identifier is in backup-info.json. If another iPad’s identifier is there, for example because you replaced your iPad, Noto stops and says “This Google Drive backup belongs to another iPad.” and offers Use This iPad Instead, after asking you to confirm. Noto then rewrites backup-info.json with this iPad’s identifier and runs a full backup. Your old iPad is meant to see, on its next backup, that the backup belongs to another iPad and to stop backing up to Google Drive. Notes that are only in the old iPad’s backup stay in your Google Drive. A note that is on both iPads is updated from this one, and files its backup no longer has go to Google Drive’s trash. The folder list folders.json becomes this iPad’s, and if it differs, the previous one is kept as folders-previous-iPad.json in the Noto Backup folder.

What stays on this iPad: Google’s sign-in library keeps its own sign-in token in this iPad’s keychain; Noto does not store it. Noto keeps your Google account’s user ID and email address in its settings on this iPad, and a small file, drive-index.json, in Noto’s own container, with the IDs, sizes and checksums of the Drive folders and files Noto made, the paths it wrote them to (PDF paths use your note names) and the addresses of unfinished uploads, so that it need not look everything up again. It holds no note content. Sign Out of Google Drive erases the account record and that file.

Noto’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

iCloud sync

iCloud sync is optional and off until you turn it on in Settings > Connected Services. It is free, and it uses your own iCloud: Noto has no server of its own, no analytics, and we never receive or see what is in your notes. Sync is not a backup — a note you delete on one iPad is deleted on your other iPads too (it goes to Recently Deleted there), and version history stays on each iPad and is not synced.

What is stored: your notes and their pages, handwriting, typed text, images and other attachments, recordings and their transcripts, imported PDFs, your folders (with their names, colours, icons and groups), course names and codes, favourites, custom templates, the times a note was changed, which of your iPads changed it (a device identifier), and the settings a note needs to look the same on another iPad. Note titles are stored too, because they are part of the note. Do not put anything in a title, course name or folder name that you would not want stored in your iCloud. Nothing about your Noto sign-in is stored.

Where: in the private CloudKit database that Apple gives your app data in your own iCloud account, under your Apple Account. Noto’s developer and other people cannot read it through Noto. Apple’s own access is governed by Apple’s terms and privacy policy, which cover that storage, and it may count toward your iCloud storage.

When: only while sync is on. Anything marked Don’t Sync, on a note or on a folder (which covers everything inside it), is never uploaded to iCloud. Noto can hold large uploads, such as long recordings and big PDFs, until you are on Wi-Fi. If the Apple Account on the iPad changes, sync pauses: nothing is sent to or fetched from the new account until you choose, and notes that came from the old account stay on this iPad unless you confirm. Don’t Sync is about iCloud only: it does not affect Auto-Backup, which may still copy it to your Files folder or your own Google Drive.

Keeping and deleting: if you move a note to Recently Deleted, its copy in iCloud stays until you delete it permanently; deleting it permanently deletes its content from iCloud and leaves only an empty marker (a random identifier, a time and which iPad made the change). If you mark a note or folder Don’t Sync after it was synced, Noto deletes its content from iCloud and leaves the same kind of marker, so your other iPads do not mistake it for a deletion; copies already on your other iPads stay there. When you turn sync off, Noto asks whether to keep your notes on this iPad or remove them from this iPad. Notes already in iCloud keep their copy there; Remove moves them to Recently Deleted on this iPad. Notes marked Don’t Sync or not yet uploaded have no copy in iCloud. Noto deletes the records it wrote; iCloud may keep the underlying data for a while and may take time to free the space.

To delete everything Noto keeps in your iCloud, use your iPad’s iCloud storage settings (Settings > Apple Account > iCloud; the exact menu names depend on your iPadOS version, and Noto cannot promise how its data is listed there). Your notes on this iPad stay, and Noto will ask whether to upload them again. To stop sync, turn it off in Settings > Connected Services; to stop all apps using iCloud, use your iCloud settings.

Keeping and deleting your data

Noto keeps your notes, recordings and settings on this iPad for as long as you keep them there, and, if you turn on iCloud sync, a copy in your own iCloud until Noto deletes it (see iCloud sync). A note you delete moves to Recently Deleted and stays there until you recover it or delete it permanently; a recording you delete is gone from this iPad at once (see Auto-Backup to a Files folder and Auto-Backup to Google Drive for any backup copy, and iCloud sync for the iCloud copy). Version history keeps a note’s automatic saves for 7 days, and up to 50 copies saved by you or taken before a bigger change, such as deleting a page, for as long as the note exists. Deleting a note permanently removes its pages, recordings, transcripts and versions with it from this iPad; a copy Auto-Backup already made stays in your folder or your Google Drive, and if iCloud sync is on, Noto deletes the note’s content from iCloud and leaves only the empty marker described there (see iCloud sync). Deleting Noto from your iPad removes everything Noto stored in its container, but not your iCloud copy or your Google Drive copy, and apart from small records in the keychain: the sign-in record, which Noto clears the next time it is installed and opened, if you ever turned on Auto-Backup, the random identifier described under Auto-Backup to a Files folder, which Noto does not clear, and, if you ever signed in with Google or chose Google Drive, the token Google’s sign-in library keeps (Sign Out of Google Drive ends that sign-in; Noto cannot promise whether iPadOS keeps the token if you delete the app without signing out).

To withdraw a permission, turn it off in iPad Settings > Apps > Noto. To stop Auto-Backup or iCloud sync, turn it off in Settings > Connected Services; to also end the Google sign-in Noto used for Google Drive, use Sign Out of Google Drive there (see Auto-Backup to Google Drive). To stop using a sign-in, use Sign out or Remove profile in Settings > Account, and remove Noto from your Apple or Google account as described under Signing in with Apple or Google. Noto keeps no copy of your data anywhere but this iPad, any folder you chose for Auto-Backup, your own Google Drive if you chose it and, if you turned on iCloud sync, your own iCloud, which we cannot reach, so there is nothing of yours on a server of ours to delete. Copies you exported or backed up yourself, or that Auto-Backup made, stay where you put them until you delete them. Ask us to delete a support thread and we will.

Getting help

If you open a support request or post in the Noto Discord, we see what you send: your GitHub or Discord username, and whatever you describe. Support requests are public GitHub issues, so keep anything private out of them. That is correspondence, not something the app collected — it is never linked to your notes, because Noto has no way to identify them and never sends them anywhere.

Report problem with note, in a note’s menu, opens GitHub’s new-issue page in your browser with a report already written: the app version and build, the iPadOS version, the iPad model code (such as iPad16,3, not your iPad’s name), whether the note is a PDF, its page count, whether it has recordings or images, and the names of the built-in templates it uses. It never includes the note’s title or anything you wrote, and nothing is sent until you read it and submit it on GitHub.

For a problem with a purchase we may ask for an App Store order ID, because that is what Apple needs to look it up — send it privately in the Noto Discord, never in a public support request. Do not send passwords; nobody here will ever ask for one.

GitHub and Discord each have their own privacy terms, which cover their side of the conversation. Ask us to delete a support thread and we will.

Purchases

Noto Plus is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and tells this app only whether the purchase is active. Noto never sees your payment details.

Buying needs a sign-in with Apple or Google, so that the purchase belongs to an account. Noto attaches to the purchase an account token: a code (a UUID) that this iPad works out from which provider you signed in with and the identifier that provider gave Noto for your account. Your email address and name are not part of it. The App Store sends the token back with the purchase’s transaction, so the purchase can be matched to the account, and a future Noto website could work out the same code from a verified sign-in. Apple receives that token with the purchase, not your email. Nothing else about you is added, and the token is not stored separately on this iPad. Restoring purchases and Noto Plus itself follow your Apple ID, not your Noto sign-in.

Children

Noto itself collects nothing from anyone, a child included; the Google sign-in described under What Noto collects is optional, and Sign in with Apple, no sign-in, leaving Google Drive unchosen, or not restoring from Google Drive avoids it. Noto shows no ads and has no social or messaging features.
